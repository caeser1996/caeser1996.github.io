Two platforms, one product: integrating an acquired HR and payroll SaaS in Southeast Asia

A Singapore-based HR and payroll software company bought a smaller Malaysian competitor for its customers and local payroll expertise. The deal assumed the two platforms would merge within a year. They had different clouds, identity systems, tenancy models and payroll engines, and both held sensitive employee data under different national laws. The goal was to integrate without losing customers, breaking payroll runs or moving data unlawfully.

The situation

A Singapore-based HR and payroll software company serving employers across Southeast Asia acquired a smaller Malaysian competitor. The buyer wanted the target's customer base and its depth in Malaysian statutory payroll. The investment case assumed one product within a year and savings from running one platform.

The technology did not line up. The buyer ran a multi-tenant platform on one cloud with its own identity system; the target ran single-tenant deployments on another cloud with a different identity provider. Each had its own payroll engine encoding years of country rules. Both held sensitive employee data: salaries, bank details, identity numbers.

Why now. Technology consolidation is accelerating, from CrowdStrike's agreed all-stock deal for Zscaler valued at about $43.9 billion1 down to many smaller software acquisitions in the region. Most deal value depends on integration, and integration is where it is most often lost. This engagement started the week after the deal closed.

What had to be true

  • No customer feels the merger as an outage, and no payroll run is wrong.
  • One product to customers early, even while two platforms run behind it.
  • Data moves lawfully between countries, with assessments on file.
  • One view of production across both clouds during the transition.
  • One engineering organisation, with clear ownership, not two teams defending their systems.

The hard part: two payroll engines that both claimed to be right

Payroll rules look deterministic until two engines disagree. Rounding, the order in which statutory deductions apply, mid-month joiners and arrears all produced differences. The team ran each migrating customer's payroll on both engines for at least one full period and compared every payslip line. Differences became tickets with an owner from the team that knew each engine best; the acquired team's Malaysian rules knowledge decided many of them, which did more for integration of the two teams than any reorganisation.

Seeing both estates at once was the second problem. The two platforms ran in different clouds and accounts. The team placed OpenTelemetry gateway collectors in each environment and sent telemetry from both into one backend, the same pattern a large travel platform describes for receiving telemetry from multiple accounts through gateway collectors4.

Integrate identity before data. One login across both products gave customers a single company to deal with months before any data moved, and took the pressure off the riskiest migrations.

Data protection shaped the migration order. Singapore's PDPA restricts transfers of personal data outside Singapore unless the receiving organisation provides comparable protection2, and Malaysia's amended PDPA replaced its whitelist with a test of whether the destination provides substantially similar or equivalent protection3. Each migration wave had a documented assessment and intra-group contract terms before data moved, and Malaysian customers whose contracts required in-country hosting stayed on in-country infrastructure. Patterns from multi-tenant SaaS design and SaaS re-architecture applied directly.

What I'd do again, and differently

Again: set up joint on-call and shared observability in the first weeks; nothing builds trust between two engineering teams faster than fixing incidents together. Differently: be involved before signing. Several integration costs, such as the single-tenant contracts with in-country hosting commitments, were knowable during technical due diligence and would have changed the plan and the price. See team topologies and technical debt without a rewrite.

FAQ

Common questions

What should be integrated first after a SaaS acquisition?

Usually identity and the customer-facing surface: one login, one admin console and a facade over both back ends. That gives customers a single product early, while the riskier data and engine migrations happen behind it.

Why run payroll in parallel before cutover?

Payroll errors are immediately visible and damaging. Running the same period on both engines and comparing every payslip line before switching catches differences in rules, rounding and statutory deductions before employees see them.

Does moving employee data between Singapore and Malaysia need special care?

Yes. Singapore's PDPA restricts transfers of personal data outside Singapore unless comparable protection is ensured, and Malaysia's amended PDPA replaced its whitelist with a test of whether the destination provides substantially similar or equivalent protection. Both need documented assessments and contractual safeguards.
