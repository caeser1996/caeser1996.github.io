Giving government teams generative AI without sending a byte out of the country

Teams across a UAE government entity wanted generative AI for drafting, summarising and search. Public chatbots were off the table because the data could not leave national infrastructure. The goal was a shared, in-country LLM service with one front door, redaction, routing and evaluation built in.

The situation

A UAE government entity had the usual pattern: enthusiasm for generative AI in every department, a handful of disconnected pilots, and a security team quietly worried about staff pasting documents into public chatbots. Policy said sensitive data could not leave national infrastructure. Nobody wanted to say no to AI; they wanted a safe way to say yes.

The national direction supports that stance. The UAE Charter for the Development and Use of Artificial Intelligence puts privacy, data security and compliance with local law alongside innovation as guiding principles2.

What had to be true

  • In-country by design: models, prompts, retrieved documents, logs and evaluation data all hosted on national infrastructure.
  • One front door, so identity, redaction and audit are enforced once rather than reimplemented by each team.
  • Known applicable rules, mapped to controls before the first use case went live.
  • Measured quality: no use case in production without an evaluation set and an owner.
  • Operable by the internal team after hand-off.

The rules question needed care. The federal Personal Data Protection Law sets aside several categories from its scope, including government data and government entities1. That does not mean anything goes: it means the entity's own data classification, emirate-level policy and sector rules carry the weight, with PDPL principles a sensible baseline. We confirmed the applicable regime with legal counsel first. More context in UAE PDPL and AI architecture and the federal authority for AI and data.

Architecture

Serving used vLLM, whose PagedAttention design manages the KV cache in pages to reduce memory waste and raise throughput on shared GPUs3. That matters when a fixed, in-country GPU budget has to serve many departments. See LLM inference serving with vLLM and where to run models in the UAE.

The data flow, end to end

The hard part: one platform, many trust levels

The engineering was not the hard part. The hard part was that a single shared service had to respect different data classifications, department boundaries and risk appetites. The OWASP Top 10 for LLM applications lists prompt injection and sensitive information disclosure among the leading risks4, and on a shared platform both translate into one question: can a user see something through the model that they could not see directly?

  • Retrieval inherits the caller's permissions; the model never sees a document the user couldn't open.
  • Each use case is registered with a classification ceiling, an owner and an approved model list.
  • Redaction is configurable per use case, not one global rule that is either too loose or too strict.
  • Tool access for agent-style use cases is explicit and minimal. See prompt injection defence for agents.

Sovereignty is not only where the GPUs sit. It is whether every prompt, document and answer can be accounted for, on infrastructure you control.

How the engagement ran

The governance loop followed the NIST AI RMF functions of govern, map, measure and manage5, adapted to the entity's own approval structure.

What I'd do again, and differently

Again: put the gateway and the audit log in before the first use case, and make departments bring their own evaluation examples. Differently: start capacity planning earlier. In-country GPU capacity is finite and slow to expand, so demand forecasting deserves the same attention as the architecture.

FAQ

Common questions

Can a government entity in the UAE use generative AI without sending data abroad?

Yes. Open-weight models can be served on in-country GPU infrastructure, with an AI gateway that handles identity, redaction, routing and logging, so prompts and documents never leave national infrastructure.

Does the UAE PDPL apply to government entities?

The federal PDPL excludes government data and government entities from its scope, among other categories. Government bodies follow their own data classification, emirate-level and sector rules, so confirm the applicable regime with counsel early.

Why put a gateway in front of the models?

A gateway gives one place to enforce authentication, redaction, quotas, routing and audit logging, so controls do not depend on every application team implementing them correctly.
