High-impact by design: running AI candidate screening under South Korea's AI Basic Act

A South Korean recruitment platform used AI to match candidates to roles and summarise applications for employers. Under the AI Basic Act, AI used in hiring sits in the high-impact category. The goal was to keep the efficiency while making every AI-assisted step transparent, overseen by people and evidenced.

The situation

A South Korean recruitment platform served employers and job seekers. It used machine learning to match candidates to roles and, more recently, a language model to summarise applications and draft screening notes for recruiters. Employers liked the speed. Nobody had written down which steps used AI, how they were tested, or what a candidate could do if they disagreed.

Why now. South Korea's AI Basic Act took effect on 22 January 202612. Commentary describes core transparency duties for generative and high-impact AI, with high-impact uses, including areas such as hiring, bringing duties around risk management and human oversight23. Observers note the Act tries to balance innovation with social risk, and that its details are being filled in through implementing rules4. With enforcement maturing and enterprise customers asking for assurances, the platform chose to build for the high-impact obligations now.

What had to be true

  • Every AI use known and classified, with hiring uses treated as high-impact.
  • Candidates informed when AI is involved, with a route to human review.
  • A person decides: AI can rank and summarise, but no rejection without a recruiter's decision.
  • Models tested before release, including for differences across groups of candidates.
  • Evidence on demand for any application: inputs, outputs, model version and decision.

Architecture

The data flow, end to end

The hard part: evidence without slowing recruiters down

Oversight fails when it becomes paperwork. The platform made evidence a by-product of normal work: every AI call was traced with OpenTelemetry's GenAI conventions, capturing the input, the output, the tools used and the model, so any application could be reconstructed later5. The reviewer workbench put the AI summary next to the original CV with citations, so checking it took seconds rather than minutes.

In high-impact AI, the log is part of the product. If you cannot reconstruct a decision, you cannot defend it, improve it or explain it to the person it affected.

Personal data in traces was minimised and access-controlled; summaries were stored with the application, not in a separate analytics copy. Evaluation followed the approach in evaluation sets that predict production, with checks for differences across candidate groups added to the release gate. See also LLM observability with OpenTelemetry.

What I'd do again, and differently

Again: build the inventory and tracing first; everything else depended on them. Differently: design the candidate-facing review process earlier. Explaining an AI-assisted outcome in plain language was harder than producing the technical evidence. For broader governance context, see an AI governance programme across jurisdictions and ISO 42001 vs NIST AI RMF.

FAQ

Common questions

Is AI used in hiring high-impact under Korea's AI Basic Act?

Commentary on the Act describes high-impact AI as including uses in areas such as hiring and lending, which bring duties around risk management, transparency and human oversight. Check the Act and its Enforcement Decree for the exact scope.

When did the AI Basic Act take effect?

South Korea's AI Basic Act took effect on 22 January 2026.

What evidence should a hiring platform keep for AI decisions?

For each application: which model and version ran, what it was given, what it produced, what the reviewer saw and decided, and the evaluation results for that model version. Tracing AI calls makes this a by-product of normal operation.
