Same assistant, different model: launching an AI shopping assistant in China for a foreign consumer brand

A European consumer brand had an AI shopping assistant that answered product questions and built routines for customers in Europe and the US. China was its fastest-growing market, and the global assistant could not simply be switched on there. The task was to launch a Chinese edition that met local rules, ran on a locally permitted model and kept customer data in China, without forking the product.

The situation

A European skincare and personal care brand sold online and through partners in Europe, the US and China. Its AI shopping assistant answered questions about ingredients and products, built routines from a short questionnaire and handed off to customer service when needed. It ran on a global model provider, with logs and customer profiles in the brand's European cloud. China had its own app, mini-programme and marketplace stores, but no assistant.

Why now. On 31 October 2026, Apple and Alibaba said Alibaba's Qwen will power the China edition of Apple Intelligence, a clear signal that even global platforms ship a different model in China to meet local rules1. China's Interim Measures for generative AI services require qualifying public-facing services to go through filing with the Cyberspace Administration of China2, which publishes lists of filed services in batches3. And the Personal Information Protection Law governs any transfer of Chinese customers' data abroad, with certification measures in effect from 1 January 2026 alongside the standard contract and security assessment routes4. The brand's leadership wanted a Chinese edition for the next sales season, built the right way.

What had to be true

  • A locally permitted model for Chinese users, with the application registered where required.
  • Personal data stays in China: prompts, answers, profiles and logs.
  • One product, not two: shared code and features, with market-specific configuration.
  • Chinese-language quality proven on a local evaluation set before launch.
  • Documentation that follows the design: filing, impact assessment and transfer analysis drawn from the architecture.

Architecture

The data flow, end to end

The hard part: one product, two data worlds

The tempting path was a separate Chinese build: copy the code, swap the model, deploy in China. That would have doubled every future feature and fix. Instead the team moved the product to a task-level AI interface. The application asks for "answer a product question" or "build a routine", and a router decides which model serves it, based on the user's market. Prompts and safety rules became configuration per market, so Chinese prompts could reflect local language, product names and expectations without forking code. See designing AI products with a model per market.

Data was the second half. Product catalogue data is not personal, so it is synced into China. Customer profiles, conversations and logs are created and stored in China and never leave. Telemetry was the easiest thing to get wrong: the global observability stack would have pulled Chinese traces out of the country by default. The team ran a separate telemetry pipeline inside the China environment, using a shared collector with separate pipelines per channel to keep operations simple5, and only aggregated, non-personal metrics are reported to the global dashboard.

Keeping personal data in China also simplified the legal work. With nothing personal designed to leave the country, the assistant did not rely on any of PIPL's transfer mechanisms, a position counsel reviewed, and the impact assessment could describe a clean boundary rather than a set of exceptions4.

Design the boundary first and the paperwork follows. Bolt on compliance after launch and the paperwork becomes the design.

What I'd do again, and differently

Again: refactor to a task-level interface before building the China region. It turned a risky fork into configuration. Differently: build the Chinese evaluation set with the China customer service team from day one; their real questions were the best test material. FAQ

Common questions

Can a foreign company launch a generative AI assistant in China?

Yes, but public-facing generative AI services are subject to China's Interim Measures and a filing process with the Cyberspace Administration of China. Building on a model that has already completed filing, and registering the application where required, is the common route.

Can Chinese customer data be processed outside China?

Only under PIPL's cross-border transfer rules, which use a security assessment, standard contract or certification depending on volumes and data types. Keeping personal data in China avoids most of that complexity.

Does this mean maintaining two separate products?

Not if the product calls tasks rather than a specific model. A router can send Chinese users to a locally permitted model and keep their data in China, while the rest of the product stays shared.
