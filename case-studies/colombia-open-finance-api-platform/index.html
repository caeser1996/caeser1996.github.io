From voluntary to mandatory: an open finance platform for a mid-sized Colombian bank

Colombia has made open finance mandatory for supervised financial institutions. A mid-sized bank had to become both a reliable data provider and a capable data receiver, on standards still being written, without building twice. The answer was a platform designed around consent, security profiles and measurable service levels.

The situation

A mid-sized Colombian bank offered current accounts, cards and small business loans. It had taken part in the earlier, voluntary open finance scheme in a limited way: a few partner integrations, each built separately, with its own consent screens and its own security setup. Customer data lived in a core banking system, a card platform and a lending system, each with a different idea of what a "customer" was.

Why now. Decree 368 of 2026 made open finance mandatory for institutions supervised by the Financial Superintendence of Colombia (SFC), replacing the voluntary framework1. Supervised entities must act as both data providers and data receivers, and data can only flow with the customer's prior, express and informed consent2. The common technical standards are left to the SFC, and commentary on the decree sets out a deadline in October 2026 for the SFC to publish its schedule for them, with a period for institutions to comply after each standard is issued3. The bank had to start building before the details were final.

What had to be true

  • One consent model for every data flow, out and in, that customers can see and revoke.
  • Standards-ready APIs: a canonical internal model that can be mapped to the SFC standards as they arrive.
  • Financial-grade security for every third party, with no bespoke partner setups.
  • Measurable reliability: availability and latency per endpoint, because the regulator will publish indicators.
  • Useful received data: data from other institutions available to credit and product teams, with purpose recorded.

Architecture

The data flow, end to end

The hard part: building to standards that do not exist yet

The decree sets the framework, but the technical standards come later13. Waiting meant missing the window; building to a guess meant rework. The team separated what was certain from what was not.

Security was the most certain. Open finance schemes around the world converge on financial-grade API profiles, so the authorisation server was built to the OpenID Foundation's FAPI 2.0 Security Profile, with sender-constrained tokens and strong client authentication4. Whatever the local profile adds, most of that work carries over.

Data formats were the least certain. Instead of exposing internal systems directly, the team built a canonical model of customers, accounts, cards and loans, fed by change data capture from each source system. The public API shapes became a thin mapping layer on top. When the SFC publishes a standard, the change lands in the mapping, not in three core systems. More on that pattern in change data capture and event streams and data contracts.

Reliability had to be measurable from day one, because the SFC must publish indicators on the system2. The gateway emits a trace for every call, so availability and latency can be reported per endpoint and per participant, and a slow downstream system shows up in the trace rather than in a complaint5.

When the rules are still being written, build the parts every scheme shares, consent, security and measurement, and keep the parts that will change behind a thin mapping layer.

What I'd do again, and differently

Again: treat the receiver side as a product, not a compliance task. Data from other institutions is what makes open finance worth the cost, as the Brazilian lender found when it used consented data in credit decisions. Differently: start participant onboarding earlier. Technical integration was faster than agreeing testing and support arrangements with other institutions. FAQ

Common questions

Is open finance mandatory in Colombia?

Yes. Decree 368 of 2026 makes participation mandatory for entities supervised by the Financial Superintendence of Colombia (SFC), both as data providers and data receivers, replacing the earlier voluntary scheme. Data can only be shared with the customer's prior, express and informed consent.

Have the technical standards been published?

The decree leaves the common, mandatory standards to the SFC. Commentary on the decree sets out a deadline in October 2026 for the SFC to publish its schedule for issuing them. Banks should design so that the final standards can be mapped in without rebuilding.

Why use a financial-grade API security profile before the standards are final?

Profiles such as FAPI 2.0 are widely used by open finance schemes and cover the hard parts, such as sender-constrained tokens and strong client authentication. Building to one early makes it likely that most of the work carries over, whatever the final local profile says.
