Access by design: building an EU Data Act-ready data platform for a maker of connected equipment

A European manufacturer of connected heating and ventilation equipment collected rich product data, but only its own service teams could use it. The EU Data Act turned that into an obligation: users must be able to access the data their products generate and share it with others. The goal was to build that into the product platform rather than bolt on a support process.

The situation

A manufacturer of connected heat pumps and ventilation units sold across the European Union. Its devices streamed temperatures, energy use, fault codes and runtime data to the cloud, where service teams used it for diagnostics and the product team used it to improve designs. Homeowners, installers and energy advisers saw only a slice of it in an app, and there was no way for a customer to take their data to someone else.

Why now. The EU Data Act gives users rights over the data their connected products and related services generate, including access and sharing with third parties they choose12. Its access-by-design obligation applies to connected products placed on the market after 12 September 2026, and commentators stress that it is an engineering obligation that has to be built into the product3. Separately, the Act's cloud switching rules prohibit switching charges from 12 January 20273. For a product line launching new models, both dates landed in the middle of the roadmap.

What had to be true

  • Users can get their data easily, in a usable format, without raising a support ticket.
  • Users can share it with an installer, energy adviser or app of their choice, and revoke that.
  • Personal data handled under the GDPR, because household energy patterns can identify people4.
  • Trade secrets protected through agreed measures, not used as a blanket excuse.
  • No cloud lock-in in the platform itself, in line with the switching rules.

Architecture

The data flow, end to end

The hard part: ingestion that scales with the fleet

Access by design starts at ingestion: if data is dropped, summarised too early or locked into a proprietary pipeline, users cannot get it later. The team standardised device telemetry on OTLP and placed a tier of OpenTelemetry Collectors in gateway mode behind a load balancer in each region, so data keeps flowing as the fleet grows and individual collectors can be replaced without loss5.

You cannot share data you never kept. Access by design is decided at ingestion, years before a user asks.

The second hard part was the line between raw data and know-how. Raw readings went into the user-accessible store; the predictive maintenance models and efficiency algorithms built from them stayed separate, with sharing partners bound by terms to protect anything sensitive. More on related patterns in OpenTelemetry Collector deployment patterns and data contracts.

What I'd do again, and differently

Again: treat the catalogue as a product, because it made the data useful to customers and partners, not just compliant. Differently: settle the trade secret approach with legal before building the stores, since it shaped the data model more than any technical choice. FAQ

Common questions

What does the EU Data Act require for connected products?

For connected products and related services placed on the EU market after 12 September 2026, product data must be designed to be accessible to users, and users can ask for it to be shared with third parties of their choice. Personal data in that data remains subject to the GDPR.

Can a manufacturer refuse to share data to protect trade secrets?

The Data Act allows trade secrets to be protected through agreed measures, and only in limited cases to refuse or suspend sharing. Designing the data model to separate raw product data from proprietary derived models makes this much easier.

How does the Data Act affect cloud contracts?

It includes rules to make switching between data processing services easier, and switching charges, including data egress charges for switching, are prohibited from 12 January 2027.
