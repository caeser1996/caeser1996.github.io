When the customer is an agent: making a Southeast Asian travel marketplace ready for AI assistants

A regional travel marketplace saw consumer AI agents arrive in its home market, able to compare, basket and book on a person's behalf. Its site was built for humans and scrapers. The goal was to give agents a fast, safe and honest way in, without breaking the experience for everyone else.

The situation

A travel marketplace serving Singapore and its neighbours sold hotels, flights and activities through a website and app. Its traffic already included price scrapers and comparison bots, handled with blocking and rate limits. Its booking flow assumed a person clicking through screens.

Why now. In late October 2026, Meta made its personal AI agent, Muse, available in Singapore, with partners including Shopee, Grab, Singapore Airlines, Agoda and Trip.com12. Coverage describes the agent building a basket and waiting for the person's approval before ordering, and asking for approval before sensitive steps such as purchases2. For a travel business, that means some customers will arrive through an assistant rather than the app, and the marketplace either offers agents a good way in or watches them book elsewhere. See the tech news update for 29 October.

What had to be true

  • Agents read the truth: inventory, total prices, fees and cancellation rules available as structured data, not scraped from pages.
  • No booking without a person's approval, and no duplicate bookings from retries.
  • Agent traffic isolated, so a busy agent platform cannot slow down human customers.
  • Clear data protection basis for using personal data in recommendations under Singapore's PDPA.
  • One interface for many agents, not a custom integration per assistant.

Architecture

The data flow, end to end

The agent-facing tools followed the Model Context Protocol so different assistants could use the same interface5. Security for those tools followed the practices in securing MCP servers, and the idempotent booking design reused patterns from idempotent payment pipelines.

The hard part: serving agents without hurting people

Agent platforms can generate bursts of searches as they compare options for thousands of users at once. The answer was to route agent traffic to its own backends with its own limits and capacity, so a spike from one assistant never slowed the app. The Kubernetes Gateway API made this declarative: routes can match on headers, such as an authenticated agent identifier, and split or direct traffic to different services4.

Treat agents as a customer channel, not as bots to block. Give them a front door, rules and limits, and they become measurable revenue instead of unmanaged load.

On personal data, the PDPC's advisory guidelines explain how consent and notification obligations, and their exceptions, apply when personal data is used in AI recommendation systems3. Recommendations to agents used only the data needed for the request, and anything personalised was tied to a recorded basis. More on the rules in fractional CTO in Singapore.

What I'd do again, and differently

Again: make prices and policies machine-readable first; it improved the human site too. Differently: involve finance and customer service earlier. Approval, refund and dispute flows for agent bookings raised more questions than the APIs did. Related reading: structured data for AI search and MCP registries for enterprises.

FAQ

Common questions

What does agent-ready mean for an online marketplace?

Agents can find, compare and book through documented, machine-readable interfaces instead of scraping pages, with clear prices and policies, safe booking steps that need human approval, and their own rate limits and monitoring.

Should we block AI agents or serve them?

Blocking rarely works and loses customers who choose to shop through an assistant. Serving agents through a proper interface gives you control: authentication, limits, accurate data and a human approval step for purchases.

How do you stop an agent from booking twice?

Make every booking request idempotent with a client-supplied key, hold inventory for a short approval window, and confirm only after an explicit approval from the person the agent is acting for.
