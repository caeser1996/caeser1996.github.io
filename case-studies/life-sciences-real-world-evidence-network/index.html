Building a reproducible real-world evidence network across external data partners

A pharmaceutical evidence team licensed data from several partners and started every study with a new data pull and months of cleaning. The goal was a study pipeline that ran the same pre-specified design at every partner, measured its own bias, and could be re-run exactly when a regulator asked.

The situation

A life sciences evidence team answered questions about treatment patterns, comparative safety and outcomes using data licensed from several external partners: claims databases, EHR-derived datasets and a disease registry. Each study started the same way: negotiate an extract, receive files in the partner's format, spend months cleaning and harmonising, then write analysis code that only worked on that one extract. When a reviewer later asked a question, re-creating the original analysis was an archaeology project.

The partners were also reluctant to send patient-level data at all. The team needed a way to run studies where the data lived, with results the organisation would be comfortable defending to a regulator.

What had to be true

  • One common model at every partner, so study code runs unchanged.
  • Design before data: protocols, cohorts and analyses pre-specified and locked before results are seen.
  • Bias measured, not assumed, using controls with a known answer.
  • Exact reproducibility of any result, months or years later.
  • Data stays with the partner; only reviewed aggregates come back.

The FDA's framework for its real-world evidence programme frames the questions such evidence will face, including whether the data are fit for use and whether the study design can provide adequate evidence1. Those questions shaped the architecture from the start.

Architecture

Analytics used OHDSI's HADES packages, open-source R tools for characterisation, population-level effect estimation and patient-level prediction against the OMOP CDM2. Partner conversions followed the patterns in FHIR to OMOP architecture and OMOP vocabularies where partners started from scratch.

Cohort practice is covered in OMOP cohort definitions, and study design choices in real-world evidence study designs on OMOP.

The hard part: proving the design is not fooling you

Observational studies can produce confident, wrong answers. OHDSI's approach to method validity uses negative controls, exposure and outcome pairs believed to have no causal effect, to measure residual systematic error, and empirical calibration to adjust confidence intervals and p-values for it3. Making that routine required:

  • A curated negative control list per study, reviewed by clinical scientists rather than generated and forgotten.
  • A rule that effect estimates stay blinded until diagnostics (covariate balance, equipoise, negative control distribution) pass.
  • Diagnostics reported per partner, because bias that appears in one data source and not another is itself a finding.

The most valuable output of a negative control analysis is permission to say "this design should not be trusted here". Building that into the process is what makes the other results credible.

How the engagement ran

Execution used Strategus, which runs HADES modules from a JSON analysis specification and restores a pinned R environment, separating study design from site-specific execution settings4.

What I'd do again, and differently

Again: treat the archived study package as the deliverable, not the slide with the hazard ratio. Differently: invest earlier in partner data cards. Knowing each source's coverage, lag and coding habits up front would have saved feasibility rounds on questions some partners could never answer.

FAQ

Common questions

How is this different from a hospital research platform?

A hospital platform standardises its own data. A life sciences evidence network works with external partners who keep their data, so the focus shifts to study design, partner-side execution, diagnostics and reproducibility across many sources.

What are negative controls for?

Negative controls are exposure and outcome pairs believed to have no causal relationship. If a study design finds effects for them, that reveals systematic bias, and the estimates can be calibrated or the design rejected.

What does regulatory-grade reproducibility mean in practice?

Being able to re-run a study and get the same answer: the protocol, cohort definitions, analysis code, software environment, vocabulary and data versions are all pinned and archived together.
