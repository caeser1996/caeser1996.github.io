Decisions people can question: explainable AI credit scoring for a Brazilian digital lender

A Brazilian digital lender approved and priced loans in seconds with machine learning models. Customers could not easily find out why they were declined, and reviewing a decision meant digging through logs. The goal was to make every automated credit decision explainable, reviewable by a person and ready for the AI rules Brazil is debating.

The situation

A digital lender in Brazil offered personal loans and credit lines through its app. Machine learning models approved, declined and priced applications in seconds, using bureau data, open finance data shared with consent and in-app behaviour. Growth was strong. Complaints were the warning sign: declined customers could not get a useful answer about why, and when the team reviewed a decision it meant piecing together logs from several services.

Why now. Brazil's data protection law, the LGPD, already gives people the right to request review of decisions taken solely through automated processing that affect their interests, including credit profiling, and to receive clear information about the criteria used1. The national data protection authority, ANPD, oversees it2. On top of that, Brazil's AI bill, PL 2338/2023, passed the Senate in December 2024 and was pending in the Chamber of Deputies, with a vote reported as expected after the October 2026 elections345. It is not law yet, but a lender relying on automated credit decisions could not afford to wait.

What had to be true

  • Every decision reconstructable: model version, data used and outcome, stored together.
  • Clear reasons a customer can understand, without exposing trade secrets.
  • Human review on request, handled quickly with full context.
  • Governed models: tested before release and monitored after.
  • Data minimised and documented, especially open finance data used with consent.

The hard part: reconstructing a decision months later

A review request can arrive weeks or months after the decision. By then models have been retrained and data has changed. The team made the decision record immutable and complete at the moment of decision, and linked each application event to the processing that handled it with distributed tracing. OpenTelemetry instrumentation for Kafka links the receive and process steps of a message in one trace, so analysts can follow an application from arrival to outcome6.

An explanation you generate later is a story. An explanation recorded at decision time is evidence. Build for the second.

Reason codes were generated from the model's own attributions and mapped to plain-language statements reviewed by legal and customer teams, so explanations were truthful without revealing model internals. What I'd do again, and differently

Again: consolidate decisions into one service before adding explanations; scattered logic cannot be explained. Differently: track overturned decisions from day one. They were the clearest signal of where models or rules needed work. Related: structured outputs and where I work.

FAQ

Common questions

Does the LGPD cover automated credit decisions?

Yes. The LGPD gives data subjects the right to request review of decisions taken solely on the basis of automated processing that affect their interests, including profiling such as credit, and to receive clear information about the criteria and procedures used, subject to trade secrets.

Is Brazil's AI bill law yet?

Not at the time of writing. Bill 2338/2023 was approved by the Senate in December 2024 and was pending in the Chamber of Deputies, with a vote reported as expected after the October 2026 elections.

What are reason codes?

Short, standardised explanations of the main factors behind a decision, such as income stability or existing debt, generated from the model and the inputs it used, so customers and reviewers can understand and challenge the outcome.
