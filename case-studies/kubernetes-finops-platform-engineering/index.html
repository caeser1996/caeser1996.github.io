Making Kubernetes spend visible, owned and controllable, without slowing teams down

Cloud bills were growing faster than the business, and nobody could say which team or product drove the increase. The goal was a platform where every workload has an owner, a cost, sensible defaults and a cheap way to shrink, so cost becomes a product decision rather than a quarterly panic.

The situation

A fast-growing technology company ran most of its products on a handful of shared Kubernetes clusters. Teams shipped quickly, which was the point. But cloud spend had become one of the largest lines in the budget, and the monthly review was a guessing game: the bill showed compute, storage and network by account, while the organisation thought in teams and products. Requests and limits had been copied between services for years. Some workloads ran around the clock for jobs that happened once a day.

A previous clean-up project had cut costs for a quarter, then spend crept back. The leadership question was simple: how do we make this stay fixed?

What had to be true

  • Every workload has an owner: a team and a product, enforced, not hoped for.
  • Cost is visible where decisions are made: per team, per product, in a format finance accepts.
  • Efficiency is the default: new services start right-sized and autoscaled without teams becoming Kubernetes experts.
  • Shrinking is cheap and safe, so teams actually do it.
  • No slowdown in delivery; controls live in the paved road, not in tickets.

Architecture

The data flow, end to end

OpenCost provides workload-level cost allocation for Kubernetes, attributing cluster costs to namespaces, labels and workloads1. Joining those allocations with billing data in the FinOps Foundation's FOCUS format gave finance and engineering one view of the same numbers2; the reasoning is in FOCUS and AI spend.

The hard part: making shrinking safe

Visibility alone rarely changes behaviour. Teams knew some services were over-provisioned; they left them because changing requests meant restarts, and restarts meant risk. Two Kubernetes changes removed most of that friction:

  • In-place Pod resize, stable since Kubernetes 1.35, lets CPU and memory change on running Pods, and the Vertical Pod Autoscaler's in-place mode uses it3. Recommendations could finally be applied continuously, starting with CPU on stateless services. Details in in-place Pod resize.
  • Scale to zero with the HorizontalPodAutoscaler, available from Kubernetes 1.37 for workloads driven by suitable object or external metrics such as queue length4. Workers that waited idle most of the day stopped costing anything while idle. See Kubernetes FinOps and scale to zero.

Namespace quotas then capped the total each team could request, which turned capacity into a visible, negotiable budget rather than an unlimited pool5.

Cost programmes fail when saving money is risky and spending it is free. Flip both: make shrinking safe and automatic, and make every request visible to its owner.

How it ran

What I'd do again, and differently

Again: start with ownership and allocation, and put every improvement into the templates so it spreads automatically. Differently: agree unit cost metrics with product leaders earlier. Cost per team tells you who spends; cost per customer action tells you whether the spend is worth it.

FAQ

Common questions

How do you allocate Kubernetes costs to teams?

Label every workload with its owning team and product, enforce the labels at admission, and use a cost allocation tool such as OpenCost to apportion node and shared costs to workloads. Then report it alongside the cloud bill in a common format such as FOCUS.

Can right-sizing be done without restarting pods?

Since Kubernetes 1.35, in-place Pod resize is stable, so CPU and memory can be changed on running Pods. Combined with the Vertical Pod Autoscaler's in-place mode, recommendations can be applied with fewer restarts.

Is cost optimisation a one-off project?

It shouldn't be. One-off clean-ups decay within months. Defaults, ownership, autoscaling and regular reporting make efficient usage the normal state of the platform.
