Case study · Retail banking · Australia

Reasonable steps you can prove: preparing an Australian bank for the Scams Prevention Framework

A customer-owned Australian bank had fraud controls built for stolen cards and taken-over accounts, not for customers persuaded to send money themselves. The Scams Prevention Framework turns scam prevention into a legal duty with a regulator watching. The goal was a platform that finds scams earlier, acts on intelligence quickly and can show, payment by payment, why it did what it did.

Case study · Cloud infrastructure · Malaysia

Know your compute customer: building export-control compliance into a Malaysian GPU cloud

A Malaysian data centre operator had added a GPU cloud to serve regional AI start-ups, enterprises and research labs. Demand was strong. So was scrutiny: Malaysia had introduced permits for moving US-origin AI chips, and in November 2026 the US moved to require licences for advanced chips going to Malaysia and four other countries, including to stop restricted parties reaching them remotely. The operator needed to prove, continuously, who was using its GPUs and for what, without becoming impossible to buy from.

Case study · Payments · Brazil

Change without fear: progressive rollouts and fast rollback for a Brazilian payments platform

A Brazilian payment institution processed more than a million transactions a day, most of them instant payments that customers expect to complete in seconds. Most of its incidents were not caused by attackers or hardware, but by its own changes: a configuration tweak, a library upgrade, a network adjustment. After a major provider's long outage was traced to a routine network change exposing a latent bug, the company decided to make every change small, observable and reversible.

Case study · Medtech · European Union

Testing with the regulator in the room: an EU medtech start-up's hospital early-warning model in an AI regulatory sandbox

A European medtech start-up had built a model that warns hospital ward staff when a patient is likely to deteriorate. It worked on historical data. The questions that mattered next were harder: would it hold up on live wards, how should nurses act on it, and what would regulators expect as evidence? When the EU set common rules for AI regulatory sandboxes, the start-up decided to answer those questions in one, with a supervisor watching, without mistaking the sandbox for approval.
