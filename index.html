Case study · Medtech · European Union

Testing with the regulator in the room: an EU medtech start-up's hospital early-warning model in an AI regulatory sandbox

A European medtech start-up had built a model that warns hospital ward staff when a patient is likely to deteriorate. It worked on historical data. The questions that mattered next were harder: would it hold up on live wards, how should nurses act on it, and what would regulators expect as evidence? When the EU set common rules for AI regulatory sandboxes, the start-up decided to answer those questions in one, with a supervisor watching, without mistaking the sandbox for approval.

Case study · B2B software · United Kingdom

One login bug away: hardening single sign-on, staff access and integration keys for a UK B2B SaaS company

A UK software company sold a collaboration platform to enterprises, most of whom signed in through their own identity providers. Its engineers had built single sign-on support over years, one customer request at a time. Staff could open customer workspaces for support, and integrations held keys to code hosting and the CDN. When another SaaS company disclosed that a flaw in its SSO flow let an attacker pose as staff and reach production, the board asked whether the same chain existed here.

Central authentication serviceStrict SAML and OIDC validationSeparate staff identityJust-in-time support accessShort-lived integration tokensRead the case study →
Case study · Insurance · Singapore

When the suite goes down: keeping a Singapore digital insurer running through a productivity SaaS outage

A Singapore digital insurer ran its customer-facing platforms on its own cloud estate, with careful failover. Everything around them, email, chat, documents, identity and increasingly AI assistants, ran on one productivity suite. The insurer had planned for its own systems failing, not for that suite failing. A long outage at a major provider made the gap obvious, and Singapore's business continuity expectations for financial institutions made it urgent.

Case study · Financial services · South Korea

Inference is a server too: securing self-hosted LLM serving for a South Korean fintech

A South Korean fintech moved its AI features from external APIs to open-weight models served on its own GPUs, to keep customer data in-house and control cost. The data science team had made it work. The security team had not yet looked. When inference engines like vLLM and SGLang started appearing in advisories and breach reports, the company treated its model servers as what they are: internet-adjacent services running complex code, with customer data passing through them.

What your board needs to know about your AI: a CTO's checklist for a briefing that leads to decisions

Most boards now ask about AI, and most AI briefings either drown directors in model names or reassure them with demos. Neither helps them govern. A good briefing tells the board where AI is used, what it costs, how it could fail, which rules apply and which decisions are theirs.
