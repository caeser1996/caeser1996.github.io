Case study · Wealth management · Singapore

From guidelines to plumbing: putting MAS's AI risk management guidelines to work at a Singapore wealth manager

A Singapore wealth manager had AI everywhere and an inventory nowhere: vendor copilots, a suitability model, generative AI drafting client letters, and agents piloted by operations. MAS's final guidelines on AI risk management made the board accountable for all of it. The task was to turn the guidelines into systems that keep the inventory, the controls and the evidence current without a spreadsheet army.

Case study · Telecom · European Union

Our models, our metal: a sovereign LLM platform for a European telecom operator

A telecom operator in central Europe wanted generative AI for network operations, customer care and security, but would not send network configurations, customer records or incident data to an outside model. Capable open-weight models made running its own platform realistic. The task was to build one that is shared, secure, reliable and honest about cost.

Case study · Diagnostics · India

Results, records and research: a consent-aware data platform for an Indian diagnostics chain

A diagnostics chain ran hundreds of collection centres and several regional labs. Patients wanted results in their digital health records, doctors wanted trends, and the business wanted to use its data for research and AI. India's data protection rules arrive in phases through 2027. The platform had to make every use of a patient's data traceable to a purpose and, where needed, to consent.

Case study · Energy retail · Australia

Ready for 10 December: automated decision transparency for an Australian energy retailer

An energy retailer used software to run credit checks, set payment plans, flag hardship and trigger collections, across systems built over twenty years. From 10 December 2026, Australian organisations must explain in their privacy policy when computer programs make or substantially inform decisions that significantly affect people. The retailer first had to find out where that happened.

Analysis · Analysis · Security and financial platforms

The Binance hack: what a laptop-to-withdrawal attack teaches every platform that moves money

On 29 October, Binance reported what is being described as the largest crypto theft on record, about $6.3 billion. Its early account is familiar to anyone who runs payments: a compromised laptop, access to a service container, and withdrawal requests that the system accepted. The lessons apply to banks, fintechs and marketplaces as much as to crypto exchanges.

Analysis · Analysis · AI, search and content platforms

Reddit goes logged-in: how to protect and monetise valuable content when AI answers the search

On 29 October, Reddit said it will move towards a primarily logged-in experience, citing falling search referrals and scraping of its content for AI. It is the clearest sign yet that the open, crawlable web is being rebuilt into tiers. Any business whose content, data or listings have value now faces the same design question: who gets what, how, and on what terms?

Analysis · Analysis · Open models and sovereign AI

Mistral Large 4's open weights: when running a trillion-parameter model yourself makes sense, and when it does not

On 29 October, Mistral published the weights of Large 4, a one-trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts model pitched at security teams who want to run it on their own infrastructure. The same day, Canada announced a large equity stake in Cohere to build sovereign AI capacity. Frontier-scale open models are now a real option. Whether they are the right option is an architecture and cost question, not a headline one.
