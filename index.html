Case study · Consumer education · Canada

Safe by design: preparing a Canadian learning app's AI study companion for chatbot safety duties

A Canadian learning app gave secondary students and young adults an AI study companion. Students used it for homework, and sometimes for things that had nothing to do with homework. With Canada proposing duties for AI chatbot services, including emergency measures for crisis situations and limits on manipulative engagement, the company wanted its chatbot to be safe by design and able to show it, before any rules took effect.

Case study · Industrial software · Germany and EU

Clock-ready: Cyber Resilience Act vulnerability handling for a German industrial software maker, as AI-found reports surge

A German company sold machine-monitoring software to manufacturers across Europe: an edge gateway installed in factories and a cloud application. From September 2026 the EU Cyber Resilience Act required it to report actively exploited vulnerabilities on tight deadlines. At the same time, vulnerability reports were arriving faster, many of them written with AI. The company needed a product security process that could separate signal from noise, know which customers were exposed and start the regulatory clock at the right moment.

Case study · Software · United States

Capacity you can move: multi-region AI serving for a US SaaS company when data centres stall

A US B2B software company had built AI features that customers now relied on daily. Its model serving sat in one cloud region with one provider, on capacity that was hard to expand. With demand for AI compute outrunning supply and voters pushing back on new data centres, the company needed AI capacity it could move, without rebuilding the product each time.

Case study · Consumer goods · China

Same assistant, different model: launching an AI shopping assistant in China for a foreign consumer brand

A European consumer brand had an AI shopping assistant that answered product questions and built routines for customers in Europe and the US. China was its fastest-growing market, and the global assistant could not simply be switched on there. The task was to launch a Chinese edition that met local rules, ran on a locally permitted model and kept customer data in China, without forking the product.

Agentic infrastructure: the skills, tools, sandboxes and controls that let AI agents do real work safely

Most companies started with agents as a chat window and a few API calls. Agents that do real work need more underneath them: packaged know-how they can load when needed, tools with clear permissions, somewhere safe to run code, an identity for every action, and a record of what happened. This is the stack I design for teams moving agents from demos to production, layer by layer, with the standards that now exist for each.

Dark factories: what lights-out automation means for factories, software teams and back offices

A dark factory runs without people on the floor, so it needs no lights. The idea is decades old in manufacturing, is spreading fast with the latest wave of industrial robots, and has now been borrowed by software teams whose AI agents write, test and ship code that no human reads.
