Case study · Fintech · Indonesia

Every script runs as the user: locking down third-party code on an Indonesian e-wallet's web app

An Indonesian e-wallet's web app loaded a long list of third-party scripts for analytics, chat, performance and marketing, all running with the signed-in user's authority on pages that could move money. When a hijacked performance script drained traders on another platform in October 2026, the team asked the obvious question: what could our scripts do with our users' sessions? The answer led to a new front-end architecture.

Case study · Software services · UAE

Fast tools, short leashes: securing AI coding assistants and developer machines at a UAE software house

A software house in the UAE built systems for banks and government entities, and its engineers had adopted AI coding assistants, AI-native editors and a long tail of extensions. Then October 2026 showed how attackers use exactly those tools: a backdoored coding extension in an editor marketplace, and a run of breaches at AI developer platforms feeding a market in stolen model keys. The firm needed to keep the productivity and remove the blast radius.

Case study · E-commerce · Mexico

Answering in under a breath: voice AI agents for a Mexican e-commerce marketplace

A Mexican marketplace took most of its customer calls about one thing: where is my order. Wait times grew every sales season. Voice AI agents could answer those calls, but only if they sounded natural in Mexican Spanish, responded fast enough to feel like a conversation, handed over to people gracefully and respected callers' data rights. The architecture had to make all four true at once.

Case study · Wealth management · Singapore

From guidelines to plumbing: putting MAS's AI risk management guidelines to work at a Singapore wealth manager

A Singapore wealth manager had AI everywhere and an inventory nowhere: vendor copilots, a suitability model, generative AI drafting client letters, and agents piloted by operations. MAS's final guidelines on AI risk management made the board accountable for all of it. The task was to turn the guidelines into systems that keep the inventory, the controls and the evidence current without a spreadsheet army.
