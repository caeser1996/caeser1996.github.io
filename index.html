Capacity you can move: multi-region AI serving for a US SaaS company when data centres stall

A US B2B software company had built AI features that customers now relied on daily. Its model serving sat in one cloud region with one provider, on capacity that was hard to expand. With demand for AI compute outrunning supply and voters pushing back on new data centres, the company needed AI capacity it could move, without rebuilding the product each time.

Case study · Consumer goods · China

Same assistant, different model: launching an AI shopping assistant in China for a foreign consumer brand

A European consumer brand had an AI shopping assistant that answered product questions and built routines for customers in Europe and the US. China was its fastest-growing market, and the global assistant could not simply be switched on there. The task was to launch a Chinese edition that met local rules, ran on a locally permitted model and kept customer data in China, without forking the product.

Case study · Industrial operations · Saudi Arabia

Agents that advise, engineers who decide: AI for maintenance planning at a Saudi industrial operator

A Saudi industrial operator had decades of equipment history, a work-order system, sensor data from its plants and a backlog of maintenance decisions waiting on scarce senior engineers. AI agents could read all of it faster than any team. The challenge was to let them help without letting them touch the plant, and to keep the data in the Kingdom.

Case study · Fintech · Indonesia

Every script runs as the user: locking down third-party code on an Indonesian e-wallet's web app

An Indonesian e-wallet's web app loaded a long list of third-party scripts for analytics, chat, performance and marketing, all running with the signed-in user's authority on pages that could move money. When a hijacked performance script drained traders on another platform in October 2026, the team asked the obvious question: what could our scripts do with our users' sessions? The answer led to a new front-end architecture.

One opt-out for AI training: what the shared robots.txt token changes, and what it does not

On 30 October, OpenAI, Google, Microsoft and Anthropic said they will honour a single robots.txt signal for opting out of AI training. For anyone who publishes content, that is simpler than maintaining a list of crawler names. But rollout is uneven, the companies treat AI answers differently from training, and the signal is voluntary. Here is how to decide whether to use it, and how to check it works.
