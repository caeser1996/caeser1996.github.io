Our AI pilot won't make it to production

Symptoms

Demos impress, but answers are unreliable, costs are unknown and nobody will sign off.

Diagnosis

Usually the model isn't the problem. Retrieval is ungrounded, there's no evaluation set, and no one owns quality.

My approach
  1. Define the decisions the AI supports and the cost of a wrong answer
  2. Ground retrieval in a domain ontology and knowledge graph (GraphRAG)
  3. Build an evaluation harness and tracing before adding features
  4. Orchestrate agents with LangGraph: explicit state, tools via MCP, human checkpoints
  5. Ship one workflow to production behind guardrails, then expand
What you get
Answers with citationsMeasurable quality gatesKnown cost per queryA path from pilot to platform

Our data is everywhere and means different things

Symptoms

Every team has its own definition of a customer, a patient or revenue. Reports disagree. AI makes it worse.

Diagnosis

It's a semantic problem, not a storage problem. Without a shared model, more pipelines just copy the confusion.

My approach
  1. Run domain workshops to capture core concepts and relationships
  2. Formalise an ontology and controlled vocabularies, versioned like code
  3. Map sources onto it: entity resolution and master data
  4. Expose a semantic layer to BI, APIs and AI agents
  5. Put data contracts and quality checks at the boundaries
What you get
One definition of truthReports that reconcileAI-ready knowledge graphFaster onboarding of new sources

Our platform buckles as we grow

Symptoms

Latency spikes at peak, batch jobs overrun, incidents are frequent and every release feels risky.

Diagnosis

Synchronous coupling and shared databases turn growth into contention. The architecture is fighting the load.

My approach
  1. Instrument first: find the real bottlenecks with tracing and load tests
  2. Set SLOs and latency budgets for the critical paths
  3. Move hot paths to an event-driven core with idempotent consumers
  4. Redesign data access: indexes, aggregations, read models, caching
  5. Automate deploys and rollbacks on Kubernetes with GitOps
What you get
1M+ tx/day headroomPredictable latencyFewer incidentsSafe, frequent releases

Our healthcare data won't interoperate

Symptoms

EHRs, labs and claims arrive in different formats and codes. Every integration is a bespoke project.

Diagnosis

Point-to-point integration doesn't scale. You need a standard exchange format and a standard analytical model.

My approach
  1. Inventory sources, code systems and PHI flows
  2. Standardise exchange on HL7 FHIR; normalise to SNOMED, LOINC and RxNorm
  3. Land analytics in OMOP CDM for reproducible research with OHDSI tools
  4. Build in de-identification, consent, encryption and audit trails
  5. Layer clinical AI only on governed, provenance-tracked data
What you get
Integrations in weeks, not monthsResearch-ready datasetsHIPAA-aligned by designTrusted clinical AI

Cloud costs and complexity are out of control

Symptoms

Environments were hand-built, nobody knows what's running, and the bill grows faster than revenue.

Diagnosis

Without infrastructure as code and cost visibility, every environment drifts and waste is invisible.

My approach
  1. Map every resource to an owner, a service and a cost
  2. Codify infrastructure in Terraform modules across AWS, Azure and GCP
  3. Consolidate workloads onto a shared Kubernetes platform
  4. Right-size, autoscale and route AI calls to the cheapest capable model
  5. Report unit economics per transaction, tenant and token

Why the ontology sits in the middle

Applications change every year; what a "patient", "order" or "account" means should not. Put meaning at the core and you can swap models, vendors and frameworks without rewriting the business.

Why AI sits on top of knowledge

Agents grounded in a knowledge graph cite their sources, respect permissions and fail visibly. That's the difference between a chatbot and a system you can audit.

Why the rails run the full height

Security, compliance and operability bolted on later cost the most. Built in from day one, they become a delivery accelerator.

Approach

How I work with leadership teams.

Senior technical judgement is mostly about what not to build. These principles shape every engagement.

i.

Model the domain first

A clear ontology of the business outlives any framework. Get the nouns and relationships right and the systems, analytics and AI fall into place.

ii.

Architecture is a business decision

Every technical choice is tied to cost, risk, speed or capability, and explained in those terms to the people who fund it.

iii.

Prove it in production

Thin vertical slices, shipped early, measured honestly. Demos persuade; production systems with evaluations and telemetry decide.

iv.

Own your stack

Avoid lock-in where it matters. Portable infrastructure, open standards and data you control keep your options open.

v.

Leave the team stronger

Documentation, decision records and mentorship are deliverables. Success is the team running it confidently without me.

vi.

Discretion, always

Your roadmap, data and challenges stay yours. Confidentiality is the default.

FAQ

        
Common questions.

      

      
If yours isn't here, ask me directly. I'm happy to answer before any engagement.

    

    
What does a fractional CTO do?
A fractional CTO provides executive-level technology leadership part-time. I own technical strategy, architecture decisions, hiring and board-level communication, without the cost of a full-time hire, and hand over cleanly when you're ready for one.
Can you help us get our AI or LLM pilot into production?
Yes. I ground retrieval in a domain ontology and knowledge graph (GraphRAG), orchestrate agents with LangChain and LangGraph, and add evaluation, tracing and guardrails so quality and cost are measurable before you scale.
Do you work with healthcare data and HIPAA?
Yes. I design HIPAA-aligned architectures and work with HL7 FHIR, OMOP CDM and OHDSI, and clinical vocabularies like SNOMED CT, LOINC and RxNorm, with de-identification, consent and audit trails built in.
What is ontology engineering and why does it matter for AI?
An ontology is a formal model of the concepts in your business and how they relate. It gives every system, report and AI agent the same meaning, which makes data reconcile and makes AI answers accurate, explainable and permission-aware.
How do engagements start?
With a free 30-minute introductory call, which you can book directly from my calendar. Most clients then begin with a 2 to 4 week architecture and AI readiness review that produces a prioritised roadmap, then continue as fractional CTO, a build-and-hand-off project or advisory.
Do you work remotely with international teams?
Yes. I'm based in the United States and the UAE and work with teams worldwide, remotely and with on-site sessions when they add value.
