Case study · E-commerce · India

One GPU, many models: consolidating small AI models for an Indian multilingual marketplace

An Indian online marketplace served buyers and sellers in many Indian languages. Behind its search, listings and support sat a growing collection of small AI models: embedding models, rerankers, classifiers and fine-tuned language variants. Each had been given a whole GPU, most of which sat idle. As new GPU partitioning options reached mainstream cloud services, the company set out to serve the same models on far less hardware, without one model's traffic slowing another.

Case study · Financial services · South Africa

Every key has an owner: machine and AI agent identity for a South African fintech

A South African investment and payments platform had tightened staff logins years ago. The credentials its software used were another matter: cloud keys in CI, database passwords in configuration, partner API keys shared between services, and now AI agents with their own tool access. After an attack elsewhere showed how quickly automated attackers turn one foothold into every reachable token, the company set out to know, own, shorten and be able to revoke every machine credential it had.

Case study · Consumer education · Canada

Safe by design: preparing a Canadian learning app's AI study companion for chatbot safety duties

A Canadian learning app gave secondary students and young adults an AI study companion. Students used it for homework, and sometimes for things that had nothing to do with homework. With Canada proposing duties for AI chatbot services, including emergency measures for crisis situations and limits on manipulative engagement, the company wanted its chatbot to be safe by design and able to show it, before any rules took effect.

Case study · Industrial software · Germany and EU

Clock-ready: Cyber Resilience Act vulnerability handling for a German industrial software maker, as AI-found reports surge

A German company sold machine-monitoring software to manufacturers across Europe: an edge gateway installed in factories and a cloud application. From September 2026 the EU Cyber Resilience Act required it to report actively exploited vulnerabilities on tight deadlines. At the same time, vulnerability reports were arriving faster, many of them written with AI. The company needed a product security process that could separate signal from noise, know which customers were exposed and start the regulatory clock at the right moment.

Coding agents and secrets: what Claude Code's 'don't ask again' leak teaches engineering teams

Security firm Apiiro found real passwords and API keys in public copies of a Claude Code settings file, saved there when developers approved commands with "don't ask again". The specific fix is simple. The wider lesson is that coding agents create new files, on every developer's machine, that can hold credentials, and most secret controls were not designed with them in mind.

OpenSSH sshole: what an exploited Alpine login bypass says about the images you run

CVE-2026-123158, nicknamed sshole, let attackers log in over SSH without a password to accounts that were never meant to accept one, on Linux systems built on the musl C library without PAM, most notably Alpine. A fix shipped on 5 November and exploitation was reported within hours. The patch is simple. The lessons about base images, implicit assumptions and SSH in places it does not belong are worth more.
