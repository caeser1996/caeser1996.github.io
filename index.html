Case study · Diagnostics · India

Results, records and research: a consent-aware data platform for an Indian diagnostics chain

A diagnostics chain ran hundreds of collection centres and several regional labs. Patients wanted results in their digital health records, doctors wanted trends, and the business wanted to use its data for research and AI. India's data protection rules arrive in phases through 2027. The platform had to make every use of a patient's data traceable to a purpose and, where needed, to consent.

Case study · Energy retail · Australia

Ready for 10 December: automated decision transparency for an Australian energy retailer

An energy retailer used software to run credit checks, set payment plans, flag hardship and trigger collections, across systems built over twenty years. From 10 December 2026, Australian organisations must explain in their privacy policy when computer programs make or substantially inform decisions that significantly affect people. The retailer first had to find out where that happened.

Case study · Social housing · United Kingdom

Faster repairs, fair decisions: AI agents for a UK housing association under the new automated decision rules

A housing association received thousands of repair requests a month by phone, web and email, and missed hazards hidden in the backlog. Damp and mould now carry legal deadlines, and the UK's rules on automated decisions have changed. The aim was to use AI agents to read, classify and route every request quickly, while keeping people in charge of decisions that affect tenants.

Case study · Retail · Japan

Ready before it bites: AI data governance for a Japanese retail chain under the amended APPI

A Japanese retail chain used loyalty data to forecast demand and store cameras to understand footfall, with several contractors doing the analytics. Japan's amended privacy law is now on the books, adding surcharges, rules for facial data and a route for using personal data in statistics and AI development. The duties are not in force yet. The aim was to make the data estate ready before they are.

Mistral Large 4's open weights: when running a trillion-parameter model yourself makes sense, and when it does not

On 29 October, Mistral published the weights of Large 4, a one-trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts model pitched at security teams who want to run it on their own infrastructure. The same day, Canada announced a large equity stake in Cohere to build sovereign AI capacity. Frontier-scale open models are now a real option. Whether they are the right option is an architecture and cost question, not a headline one.

Analysis · Analysis · AI regulation in the United States

New York's youth chatbot law: what teams shipping AI assistants need to build, and by when

On 28 October, New York signed a law that bars AI chatbot features judged unsafe for minors, from pretending to be human to fostering emotional dependency. It follows California's own chatbot law by about a month. If your product talks to the public, even as a general assistant, this is now an engineering problem: knowing who might be a minor, controlling what the model does for them, and proving it.
