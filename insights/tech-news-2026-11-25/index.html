Tech news, 25 November 2026: CrowdStrike and Zscaler, the FCA on AI agents, stolen Azure API Management secrets and a $44 billion GPU financing

CrowdStrike agreed to buy Zscaler in an all-stock deal valued at about $43.9 billion, the largest cybersecurity acquisition announced. The UK's Financial Conduct Authority told builders of personal AI agents that handling money can require authorisation. Microsoft closed a flaw in Azure API Management Classic that exposed stored secrets, and a $44 billion debt financing for SpaceX's GPUs showed how AI hardware is now being funded.

This brief covers stories from 21 to 24 November.

1. CrowdStrike agrees to buy Zscaler

CrowdStrike agreed to acquire Zscaler in an all-stock deal valued at about $43.9 billion, with each Zscaler share converting into 1.135 CrowdStrike shares; Zscaler's founder will continue to lead the Zscaler business and join CrowdStrike's board1. The deal needs shareholder and regulatory approval and is expected to close in the second half of CrowdStrike's fiscal 20282.

CTO take: endpoint security and zero-trust network access from one vendor will be pitched as simpler and cheaper. It may be, but it also concentrates risk: one vendor's outage or breach would now touch both your devices and your network access. If you use either product, nothing changes before closing; use the period to check contract terms, renewal dates and how hard it would be to replace each part separately. See vendor admin access as perimeter.

2. The FCA warns builders of money-moving AI agents

The UK Financial Conduct Authority said companies building personal AI agents for UK consumers must consider whether their agents carry out regulated activities, such as initiating payments, arranging borrowing or making investment decisions, and may need authorisation; describing a business as a technology provider does not exempt it3. The FCA also said regulated firms remain responsible for AI systems bought from third parties4.

CTO take: for anyone building agents that touch money in the UK, the design question is now a regulatory one: which actions can the agent take on its own, and which need a regulated party? Map every agent action to whether it is advice, a payment or a credit decision, keep humans or authorised partners in those steps, and log agent decisions so you can show what happened. Banks deciding whether to accept agent-initiated payments face the mirror question. See the AI controls for a UK payments firm, agentic commerce and my UK page.

3. A flaw in Azure API Management Classic exposed stored secrets

Microsoft said a flaw in the Classic tiers of Azure API Management let an unauthorised party reach secrets stored in customer instances, and has closed the path; Norway's National Security Authority linked the activity to attacks on energy companies and advised treating every stored secret and named value as exposed5. Fixing the flaw does not revoke credentials that were already taken.

CTO take: if you run Developer, Basic, Standard or Premium Classic tiers, rotate everything stored in API Management now, starting with identity provider secrets and subscription keys, including keys for AI model back ends. Then move secrets to Key Vault references so the gateway holds pointers, not values. The broader lesson: API gateways have quietly become secret stores, and they deserve the same protection as a vault.

4. A $44 billion debt deal funds SpaceX's GPUs

Apollo and Goldman Sachs led a $44 billion notes financing to fund SpaceX's purchase of Nvidia GPUs, DCD reports, with hardware delivery planned for the first half of 20276.

CTO take: AI capacity is increasingly financed like aircraft or property, with long fixed payments regardless of how the hardware performs. That pushes providers to fill capacity with long contracts, which can mean better prices for buyers willing to commit, and more pressure on those who are not. Before signing multi-year AI capacity deals, model the cost if your usage or the hardware generation changes. See AI capacity contracts.

What I'd do this week

  • Azure users: confirm API Management tiers and rotate stored secrets if you run Classic.
  • UK teams building agents: map each agent action to regulated activities before launch.
  • Security buyers: note renewal dates and exit terms for endpoint and network access contracts.
  • Capacity planners: stress-test multi-year AI commitments against lower usage and newer hardware.
