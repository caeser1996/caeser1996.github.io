ISO/IEC 42001 or the NIST AI RMF? How to choose, and how to use both

Enterprise buyers increasingly ask AI vendors how they govern their AI. Two frameworks come up most: the NIST AI Risk Management Framework and ISO/IEC 42001. They solve different problems, and most companies will end up using both.

The security questionnaire used to ask about SOC 2. Now it often has a section on AI: how do you identify AI risks, who approves new models, how do you monitor them? Two frameworks dominate the answers, and they are often presented as alternatives. They aren't.

Two different kinds of thing

NIST AI RMFISO/IEC 42001
What it isA voluntary framework for managing AI riskA standard for an AI management system
StructureFour functions: govern, map, measure, manage1Management system clauses plus AI-specific controls2
CertificationNone; you use itCertifiable by accredited bodies
StrengthPractical risk thinking across the AI lifecycleRepeatable governance with audit and continual improvement
Typical audienceInternal teams, US government and buyersEnterprise customers and international markets wanting assurance

A common way to put it: the NIST framework is something you use, while ISO/IEC 42001 is something you certify against4. Commentators note that ISO's management-system elements, such as internal audit, management review and continual improvement, have no direct equivalent in the NIST framework3.

They map onto each other

NIST's AI Resource Center hosts a crosswalk between the AI RMF and ISO/IEC 42001. Commentators point out that it was contributed by the community rather than written by NIST, and that inclusion doesn't imply endorsement35. Informally, the four NIST functions line up with ISO's leadership and policy, context and impact assessment, monitoring, and review and improvement activities3. Treat such mappings as starting points rather than proof of coverage.

Use NIST to decide what to do about AI risk. Use ISO/IEC 42001 to prove you do it consistently. They answer different questions.

How I'd sequence them

  1. Start with an AI system register. Every framework, and every regulation, begins with knowing which AI systems you have, what they do and who owns them.
  2. Apply the NIST functions to your highest-risk systems first. Map context and impacts, measure with real evaluations, manage with controls and incident response.
  3. Build the evidence as you go: evaluation results, approval records, incident postmortems, training records. These are what auditors and customers ask for.
  4. Add the management system when customers or markets ask for certification: policy, roles, internal audit, management review.
  5. Map once to many. The same controls support the EU AI Act, SOC 2 and Gulf regimes such as DIFC Regulation 10.

Where engineering carries the load

Frameworks fail when they live only in documents. The controls that hold up in an audit are the ones built into systems: a model gateway that enforces approved models and logs every call, evaluation pipelines that gate releases as in evaluating AI agents, incident handling as in AI incident postmortems, and role-based training as in AI literacy. Build those and the paperwork follows; write the paperwork alone and it drifts from reality within months.

FAQ

Common questions

What is the difference between ISO 42001 and the NIST AI RMF?

The NIST AI RMF is a voluntary framework organised around four functions (govern, map, measure and manage) for managing AI risk. ISO/IEC 42001 is an international standard for an AI management system that organisations can be certified against.

Is there a mapping between them?

Yes. NIST's AI Resource Center hosts a crosswalk between the AI RMF and ISO/IEC 42001, which commentators note was contributed by the community rather than authored by NIST, and inclusion does not imply NIST endorsement.

Which should an AI start-up do first?

Often the NIST AI RMF, as a practical way to identify and manage risks, followed by ISO/IEC 42001 certification when customers or markets ask for external assurance. Building the risk practices first makes certification easier.
