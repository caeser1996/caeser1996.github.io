Saudi PDPL and cross-border AI: designing data flows that hold up

Many AI products serving Saudi Arabia process data with models hosted elsewhere. Since September 2024, the Personal Data Protection Law has been enforceable, and its transfer rules decide whether that design works.

A common pattern in GCC AI products: the application runs in a regional data centre, but the model, the vector database or the analytics pipeline sits somewhere else. In Saudi Arabia, that pattern now has to stand up to the Personal Data Protection Law, which became enforceable on 14 September 202435.

This is an architect's view, not legal advice. Confirm the details with Saudi counsel, and check SDAIA's current publications, since the rules have changed more than once.

The transfer rules in brief

SDAIA issued an amended Regulation on Personal Data Transfer Outside the Kingdom in September 2024, elaborating on the PDPL's transfer article1. In outline, as described by law firm commentary:

  • Adequacy and permitted purposes remain the first route, similar to the earlier version1. One firm noted in March 2025 that an adequacy list had not yet been issued4; check whether that has changed.
  • Appropriate safeguards apply where adequacy doesn't: standard contractual clauses, binding common rules and certification, with binding codes of conduct removed from the list14.
  • Standard contractual clauses come in four versions covering controller and processor combinations, and modifying them invalidates them2.
  • Risk assessments are needed when relying on safeguards or certain exemptions, and for continuous or large-scale transfers of sensitive data14. SDAIA published guidelines on conducting them in 20254.

Where AI architectures create transfers

Teams often underestimate how many cross-border flows an AI feature creates. For each one, ask whether personal data leaves the Kingdom:

ComponentHidden transfer
Hosted model APIEvery prompt containing customer data
Embedding serviceDocuments sent for vectorisation
Vector databaseEmbeddings and stored text chunks
Observability toolsPrompt and response logs, traces
Evaluation and labellingProduction samples copied for testing or annotation
Support toolingVendor staff accessing data from abroad

The model call is the transfer everyone notices. The logs, embeddings and evaluation sets are the ones that turn up in an audit.

The architecture I use

  1. Classify first. Tag data as personal, sensitive or non-personal at ingestion, so routing decisions can be automated.
  2. Keep sensitive processing in-country where possible: in-Kingdom cloud regions or on-premises models for sensitive data, which is where private LLMs earn their cost.
  3. Route through an AI gateway that redacts or blocks personal data before calls to models outside the Kingdom, and records every call with its legal basis.
  4. Keep logs local or capture metadata only, following the approach in LLM observability.
  5. Maintain a transfer register mapping each flow to its route (adequacy, SCCs, other safeguard) and its risk assessment, kept in step with the architecture.

One design for the region

Many companies serve Saudi Arabia, the UAE and the wider Gulf from one platform. The UAE PDPL, DIFC rules and the Saudi PDPL differ in detail, but the engineering controls overlap heavily: classification, in-region processing, gateways, logging and registers. I design one control set and map it to each regime, as I describe in UAE PDPL and AI architecture and DIFC Regulation 10, and pair it with the hosting choices in where to run models in the UAE.

FAQ

Common questions

When did the Saudi PDPL become enforceable?

Law firm commentary reports that the Personal Data Protection Law became enforceable on 14 September 2024, after a grace period, with SDAIA issuing further rules and guidelines around that date.

How can personal data be transferred outside Saudi Arabia?

The amended transfer regulation keeps the concepts of adequate jurisdictions and permitted purposes. Where those don't apply, appropriate safeguards such as SDAIA's standard contractual clauses, binding common rules or certification can be used, in some cases with a risk assessment.

When is a transfer risk assessment needed?

According to commentary on the amended regulation, when a controller relies on appropriate safeguards or certain exemptions, and for continuous or large-scale transfers of sensitive data. SDAIA published guidelines in 2025 on how to conduct it.
