Tech news, 18 November 2026: the Optimizely supply chain attack, Nvidia's first results without Huang, New York's AI enforcement unit and Microsoft's MAI Code 2

Attackers used a flaw in Optimizely's experimentation platform to inject malicious JavaScript into the websites of 178 organisations, a reminder that every third-party script runs with your site's full trust. Nvidia reported strong results and raised guidance in its first quarter under an interim chief executive, New York set up a unit to enforce its AI safety law, and Microsoft released cheaper coding models.

Today's brief covers stories from 16 to 17 November.

1. Attackers inject malware into 178 websites through Optimizely

Optimizely said an unauthorised party exploited a flaw in its Experimentation account sign-up flow to inject malicious JavaScript into some customer projects; Report URI identified infected snippets across 178 organisations, including major media and technology brands1. The Register reports the code targeted visitors' payment details and cryptocurrency transfers, and Optimizely says it fixed the flaw, removed the code, revoked tokens and notified customers2.

CTO take: any third-party script on your site runs with your site's full trust. List every script you load, keep testing, analytics and marketing tags off payment and login pages, restrict what scripts can do with a Content Security Policy, monitor scripts for unexpected changes and make sure you can switch a vendor's tag off in minutes. Payment card rules already require monitoring of scripts on payment pages; this shows why. See the payment page script security case study.

2. Nvidia beats and raises in its first results without Huang

Nvidia reported third-quarter fiscal 2027 revenue of $115.8 billion, with data centre revenue of $107.6 billion, and guided next-quarter revenue to about $130 billion3. CNBC notes it was the company's first report since Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive, with Colette Kress serving as interim CEO4.

CTO take: demand for AI hardware is still rising, which means capacity stays tight and expensive for most buyers into next year. Plan for that: commit to capacity only where usage is proven, make models cheaper to run, and keep your stack portable across providers. See AI capacity contracts.

3. New York creates a unit to enforce its AI safety law

New York Attorney General Letitia James announced an AI Safety, Standards, Enforcement and Transparency unit, which her office describes as the first in the country dedicated to enforcing AI laws, and which will enforce the state's RAISE Act for frontier AI developers5.

CTO take: enforcement capacity is following the laws. The direct duties fall on large model developers, but expect providers to ask customers for help with incident reporting and to update terms. Keep a record of the AI models you use and how; see the board AI briefing checklist and the US page.

4. Microsoft releases MAI Code 2 for coding

Microsoft announced MAI Code 2 and MAI Code 2 Flash at Ignite, available in GitHub Copilot, alongside other new MAI models; Microsoft's own benchmarks claim performance close to leading frontier models at a much lower price6.

CTO take: coding model choice is becoming a cost lever, not just a quality one. Test new models on your own repositories and tasks before switching, and keep your coding agent policy independent of any one model; see AI coding policy for engineering teams.

What I'd do this week

  • Inventory third-party scripts on your sites and remove any from payment and login pages.
  • Add or tighten a Content Security Policy and script change monitoring.
  • Revisit AI capacity plans in light of continued tight supply.
  • Trial new coding models on real tasks before changing defaults.

What happened in the Optimizely attack?

Optimizely said an unauthorised party exploited a flaw in its Experimentation account sign-up flow to inject malicious JavaScript into some customer projects. Report URI identified infected snippets across 178 organisations. Optimizely says it fixed the flaw, removed the code, revoked tokens and notified customers.

How can websites protect themselves from compromised third-party scripts?

Inventory every third-party script, limit what each can do with a Content Security Policy, keep scripts off payment and login pages where possible, monitor scripts for unexpected changes and be able to switch a vendor's script off quickly.

What is New York's ASSET unit?

A new unit in the New York Attorney General's office dedicated to AI safety, standards, enforcement and transparency, which will enforce the state's RAISE Act for frontier AI developers when it takes effect.
