The UAE's new Federal Authority for AI and Data: what it means for technology teams

In June 2026 the UAE created a single federal body for AI, data governance and digital government. This week Dubai set new targets for its AI campus. For companies building or selling AI in the Emirates, the institutional map is consolidating, and that changes who sets the standards you'll be measured against.

For years, AI and data policy in the UAE was spread across several bodies: an AI office within the federal government, the digital government function inside the telecoms regulator, a data office, and emirate-level and free zone authorities alongside them. Teams working here learned to ask "which regulator?" before "which rule?". That is starting to change.

This is an architect's reading of public announcements and law firm commentary, not legal advice.

What was announced

In June 2026 the UAE approved the establishment of the Federal Authority for Artificial Intelligence and Data, reporting to the Cabinet, as a unified national body for data governance, artificial intelligence and digital government12. Commentary reports that it brings together three existing functions13:

Previous bodyWhat it covered
Artificial Intelligence OfficeNational AI strategy and programmes
TDRA Information and Digital Government SectorFederal digital government services and standards
Emirates Data OfficeNational data governance

The mandate described in commentary covers the national AI strategy, government data governance, and standards and compliance across federal entities13. By October, reporting on the UAE's AI programme was already framing the authority as the body bringing national data, AI and digital government systems together6.

This week: Dubai's AI campus targets

On 26 October, during the Dubai AI Festival, Dubai approved new targets for its AI campus: more than 1,000 AI companies and over 5,000 specialised jobs by 203045. Together with federal consolidation, the direction is clear: one national voice on AI and data standards, and emirate-level competition to host the companies building on them.

Consolidation usually means fewer, clearer standards, applied more consistently. Teams that have been relying on ambiguity should plan for less of it.

What we don't know yet

  • Private sector reach. The described mandate centres on federal entities. How far its standards will extend to private companies, directly or through procurement, is not yet clear.
  • Interaction with free zones. DIFC and ADGM have their own data protection regimes, including DIFC Regulation 10 on AI systems. How federal AI standards will sit alongside them remains to be seen.
  • Data protection supervision. The federal PDPL continues to apply. Which body leads on its AI-related aspects will become clearer through guidance.
  • Health data. Sector rules, such as those in the UAE health data law, remain in place.

The regional pattern

Saudi Arabia went down this path earlier, with the Saudi Data and Artificial Intelligence Authority as a single body for data and AI7, alongside its own data protection law covered in Saudi PDPL and cross-border AI. A combined data and AI authority tends to treat data governance and AI governance as one discipline. Architecturally, that's right: most AI risk is data risk.

How I'd prepare

  1. Map your current obligations by entity and location: federal PDPL, free zone regimes, sector rules, and where your models and data actually run, as discussed in UAE AI infrastructure.
  2. Build governance on a recognised framework, such as ISO 42001 or the NIST AI RMF, so national standards become a mapping exercise.
  3. If you sell to government, expect federal data and AI standards to appear in procurement first. Keep an AI system inventory and data lineage ready to evidence.
  4. Unify data and AI governance internally. If the regulator treats them as one discipline, a separate AI committee disconnected from data owners will struggle.
  5. Watch for guidance from the new authority and plan a quarterly review of your mapping.

FAQ

Common questions

What is the UAE Federal Authority for Artificial Intelligence and Data?

A federal body announced in June 2026, reporting to the Cabinet, intended as the UAE's unified national body for data governance, artificial intelligence and digital government. Law firm commentary reports that it consolidates the Artificial Intelligence Office, the TDRA's Information and Digital Government Sector and the Emirates Data Office.

Does the new authority replace the UAE PDPL?

No. The announcements concern institutional structure and mandate. Existing laws such as the federal Personal Data Protection Law and free zone regimes such as DIFC and ADGM continue to apply; the authority's role in standards and compliance for federal entities will become clearer as it issues guidance.

What should private companies do now?

Keep complying with existing data protection and sector rules, track guidance from the new authority, and build AI governance on internationally recognised frameworks so that new national standards become a mapping exercise rather than a rebuild.
