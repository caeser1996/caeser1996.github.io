Tech news, 14 November 2026: Nvidia's CEO resigns, ChatGPT Wallet lets agents shop, Apify confirms a breach and the FBI recovers Binance funds

Nvidia's chief executive resigned, putting a leadership change at the centre of the AI hardware supply chain. OpenAI gave its agents a way to pay for things with approval each time, an automation platform confirmed customer data was accessed in an attack, and US authorities seized part of last month's record Binance theft.

Today's brief covers stories from 12 to 13 November.

1. Jensen Huang resigns as Nvidia's chief executive

Nvidia said Jensen Huang has stepped down as president, chief executive and a board member, effective immediately, and that CFO Colette Kress will serve as interim CEO while the board searches for a permanent replacement1. The resignation followed reports about Huang's conduct that Nvidia had been investigating; the board said it expects operations, customer commitments and the product roadmap to continue without interruption12.

CTO take: in the short term, chips already ordered will ship and roadmaps rarely change overnight. Over a year, a leadership change at the dominant AI hardware supplier can shift priorities, pricing and partnerships. This is a good moment to check how dependent your AI plans are on one vendor's hardware and software stack, and whether your serving layer could run on alternatives. See AI capacity contracts.

2. OpenAI launches ChatGPT Wallet for agent purchases

OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet, which lets its agents complete purchases for users: people save a card, review the items and total, and approve each purchase, while merchants and agents receive tokenised card credentials rather than the real card number3. TechCrunch reports it is rolling out to eligible US users first, and that OpenAI plans to support purchases within preset spending limits later4.

CTO take: agentic commerce is moving from demos to checkouts. If you run an online store, expect agent traffic at checkout: decide whether you will welcome or block it, make sure fraud rules do not reject legitimate agent purchases wholesale, and keep product data structured so agents describe it accurately. If you build agents, keep a human approval step for spending until you have strong limits and monitoring. See agentic infrastructure and the agent-ready marketplace case study.

3. Apify confirms customer data was accessed

Web automation platform Apify confirmed that customer data was accessed in a cyberattack, including salted password hashes, two-factor recovery codes, phone numbers and authentication tokens linked to Google and GitHub sign-ins, with limited card details, SecurityWeek reports5.

CTO take: recovery codes and sign-in tokens are as valuable as passwords. If your teams use Apify, rotate credentials and integration tokens, regenerate two-factor recovery codes and review connected GitHub and Google access. More broadly, list the automation and scraping tools that hold tokens to your code and accounts; they are part of your supply chain. See the third-party access case study.

4. US authorities seize $1.2 billion of the stolen Binance funds

The FBI said US authorities have seized more than $1.2 billion of the cryptocurrency stolen from Binance last month, which it attributes to North Korean hackers known as TraderTraitor, out of more than $2.7 billion frozen with help from crypto firms6. CyberScoop reports authorities also seized infrastructure used in a related social engineering operation7.

CTO take: recovery is welcome but partial; prevention still matters more. For anyone holding digital assets, the lessons from the Binance case remain custody design, withdrawal controls and resistance to social engineering of staff. See custody architecture lessons from the Binance hack.

What I'd do this week

  • Review how dependent your AI roadmap is on one hardware vendor's stack.
  • Decide how your checkout should treat AI agent purchases.
  • Rotate tokens and recovery codes for automation tools that have disclosed breaches.
  • List the third-party tools that hold tokens to your code repositories.
