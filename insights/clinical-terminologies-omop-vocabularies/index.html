Clinical terminologies explained: SNOMED, LOINC, RxNorm and the OMOP vocabularies

The same diagnosis can arrive as an ICD code, a SNOMED concept, a Read code or free text. Any serious health data or AI platform has to reconcile them. The OMOP Standardized Vocabularies are one of the most practical ways to do it.

Healthcare has never had a single language. Hospitals bill in one coding system, record diagnoses in another, report lab results in a third and prescribe in a fourth. Countries add their own variants. Anyone building analytics, research or AI on health data meets this problem in the first week, and it is fundamentally an ontology problem.

The main terminologies

TerminologyUsed mainly forTypical role in analytics
SNOMED CTClinical findings, conditions, proceduresRich hierarchy for clinical meaning
ICD-9/ICD-10 and national variantsDiagnoses for billing and statisticsCommon in source data; often mapped to SNOMED
LOINCLaboratory tests and observationsIdentifies what was measured
RxNormMedicationsNormalised drugs, ingredients and doses
CPT4, HCPCS and othersProcedures and servicesCommon in US claims data

Medical vocabularies go back centuries; The Book of OHDSI starts with London's Bills of Mortality. Over time each country, system and institution tended to develop its own, which is exactly what makes interoperability and multi-site research hard1.

How OMOP unifies them

The OMOP Standardized Vocabularies consolidate many vocabularies into one common format, refreshed regularly by the OHDSI vocabulary team, and they are mandatory reference tables for every OMOP CDM instance13. Organisations download them from ATHENA, which also offers search across vocabularies; proprietary vocabularies require a licence12.

The key idea is the standard concept. For each clinical meaning, one concept is designated standard and used to record data. The book's example: codes in MeSH, CIEL, SNOMED, ICD-9-CM and Read all describe atrial fibrillation, but only the SNOMED concept is standard; the others are source concepts mapped to it1. Classification concepts, such as MedDRA terms, take part in hierarchies for querying but aren't used to record data1.

Standard designation is decided per domain, not per vocabulary: conditions mainly use SNOMED, measurements SNOMED and LOINC, drugs RxNorm, RxNorm Extension and CVX1. In the book's words, there is no such thing as a "standard vocabulary".

Domains are messier than they look

Every concept is assigned a domain, such as condition, drug, procedure or measurement, which decides which CDM table it lands in. Source vocabularies often mix domains; the book shows that procedure vocabularies like CPT4 and HCPCS contain concepts from several domains1.

Mapping isn't a one-off ETL task. Vocabularies change, concepts are deprecated and remapped, and every refresh can shift results. Version the vocabulary like any other dependency.

Why this matters for health AI

  • Features and cohorts depend on it. A model trained on "diabetes" is only as good as the concept set behind it, as in OMOP cohort definitions.
  • Portability depends on it. A model built on standard concepts can be validated at another site; one built on local codes usually can't.
  • LLMs need grounding. Language models are good at suggesting codes and poor at being reliably right. Constrain them to look up concepts in the vocabulary rather than generate codes from memory, and validate outputs, in the spirit of SHACL validation for knowledge graphs.
  • Hierarchies enable better questions. Querying all descendants of a concept finds patients that a flat code list would miss.

FAQ

Common questions

What are the OMOP Standardized Vocabularies?

A consolidated collection of medical vocabularies in one common format, maintained by OHDSI and required as the reference tables of every OMOP CDM instance. They can be downloaded from ATHENA.

What is a standard concept in OMOP?

For each clinical meaning, one concept is designated standard and used to record data. For example, several vocabularies have codes for atrial fibrillation, but only the SNOMED concept is standard; the others are source concepts mapped to it.

Which vocabularies are standard for which domains?

According to The Book of OHDSI, conditions mainly use SNOMED, measurements use SNOMED and LOINC, drugs use RxNorm, RxNorm Extension and CVX, and procedures draw on several vocabularies including SNOMED and CPT4. Designation is decided per domain, not per vocabulary.
