Tech news, 17 November 2026: US licences for AI chips to five countries, a Nordic AI gigafactory consortium, AMD closes World Labs and a nervous week for AI stocks

The US moved to require licences for advanced AI chips going to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil, including to curb remote access through overseas data centres. In Europe, five companies formed a consortium to bid for an EU AI gigafactory. AMD completed its purchase of World Labs, and AI stocks fell ahead of Nvidia's results and Anthropic's expected listing.

Today's brief covers stories from 15 to 16 November.

1. The US requires licences for advanced AI chips to five countries

The Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security issued a rule requiring licences for exports of advanced AI chips and the systems containing them to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil, citing diversion risk, DCD reports1. Nextgov reports the rule also aims to stop controls being circumvented through remote access to data centres in third countries; reporting says companies headquartered in the US are exempt if they meet reporting requirements2.

CTO take: if you use or plan GPU capacity in South-East Asia or Brazil through non-US providers, expect new paperwork, delays and possibly higher prices. Ask providers how the rule affects their supply and existing contracts, and keep alternatives in other regions. If you operate data centres in these markets, know your customers and where their ownership sits. See AI capacity contracts and the Singapore page.

2. Five European firms form a consortium to bid for an EU AI gigafactory

Nokia, Nscale, Skeleton, Telia and Verda formed AI Gigafactory Northern to bid in the EU's AI gigafactory programme, proposing one AI facility spread across sites in the Nordic and Baltic region3. The Next Web reports initial sites in Finland, Estonia and Latvia, with a Swedish site to be confirmed4.

CTO take: European sovereign AI capacity is moving from policy to bids, but it will take years to arrive. If EU data residency or sovereignty matters to your customers, plan with today's providers and watch these projects as future options rather than near-term capacity. See the sovereign LLM platform case study and the Europe page.

3. AMD completes its acquisition of World Labs

AMD said it completed its acquisition of World Labs, which builds models that create and simulate interactive 3D environments, and that co-founder Fei-Fei Li joins AMD as executive vice president and chief scientist5.

CTO take: chipmakers are buying model and research teams to make their hardware more attractive. For buyers, more credible alternatives to a single accelerator vendor are good news, but only if your software stack can use them. Keep serving layers portable across hardware where you can.

4. AI stocks slide ahead of a big week

US tech stocks fell on 16 November, with AI-linked names hit hardest, ahead of Nvidia's quarterly results and Anthropic's expected market debut later in the week, CNN reports6.

CTO take: market swings rarely change engineering plans, but they shape the financing behind AI capacity and pricing. Keep contracts flexible and avoid building on a single provider whose economics you cannot see.

What I'd do this week

  • Ask GPU and AI cloud providers in South-East Asia and Brazil how the new US rule affects them.
  • Check where your AI capacity is planned and whether it relies on a single region or provider.
  • Review how portable your model serving is across hardware vendors.
