Tech news, 16 November 2026: Italy fines Meta €805 million, Microsoft explains its outage, Maileva's 22 million-person leak and a new N-able exploit

Italy's data protection authority fined Meta €805 million for accessing phone galleries without proper consent, a reminder that device permissions are personal data decisions. Microsoft gave a preliminary cause for last week's outage, a French mail platform confirmed a leak affecting 22 million people, and attackers exploited a new chain against N-able's remote management servers.

Today's brief covers stories from 13 to 15 November.

1. Italy fines Meta €805 million over Android gallery access

Italy's data protection authority, the Garante, fined Meta €805 million because the Facebook Android app's gallery suggestions feature, on by default, accessed photos, videos and metadata on users' phones without specific, informed consent, Security Affairs reports1. Meta must ask for separate consent and keep the feature off by default; it said it strongly disagrees and will appeal2.

CTO take: in the EU, reading data from a user's device needs consent even if nothing leaves the phone. Any app feature that touches photos, contacts, files or sensors should be off by default, explained in a specific prompt and logged. As AI features look for more context from devices, this ruling sets the bar; product and legal should review them together. See the Europe page.

2. Microsoft gives a preliminary cause for its outage

Microsoft said traffic shifts during routine network maintenance exposed a latent bug from an earlier capacity upgrade, causing connection requests to queue until servers ran out of processing capacity; it found no sign of malicious activity3. ARN reports the disruption lasted about 30 hours and that Microsoft will publish a full post-incident review4.

CTO take: latent bugs exposed by later changes are among the hardest failures to prevent, even for the largest operators. The lesson for your own systems is to test changes against realistic load and to make rollback fast; the lesson for your suppliers is to plan as if this will happen again. See the Microsoft 365 outage playbook.

3. A La Poste subsidiary confirms a leak affecting 22 million people

Maileva, an online mail service in La Poste's Docaposte group, confirmed on 14 November that names and postal addresses of around 22 million people were stolen in a September attack, Ouest-France reports5. The people affected were recipients of mail sent by Maileva's business customers.

CTO take: if you send letters, invoices or notices through a print-and-post provider, your customers' data sits with that provider. Include such services in your supplier inventory, ask how long they keep recipient data and whether they need to keep it at all, and prepare to answer your own customers' questions.

4. A new N-able N-central exploit chain is in use

Attackers began exploiting a new chain against N-able N-central remote management servers, dubbed 4EverAble2Shell, within hours of a proof of concept being released, BleepingComputer reports6.

CTO take: this is the second wave against N-central in a week. If your managed service provider runs it, ask for confirmation that their servers are patched or offline, and consider restricting what their remote management agents can do on your systems until they are. See the third-party access case study.

What I'd do this week

  • Review app features that read device data and make sure consent is specific and defaults are off.
  • Read Microsoft's post-incident review when published and update your outage plan.
  • Add print, mail and notification providers to your supplier inventory.
  • Get written confirmation from your managed service provider about N-central.
