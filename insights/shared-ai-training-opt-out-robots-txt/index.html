One opt-out for AI training: what the shared robots.txt token changes, and what it does not

On 30 October, OpenAI, Google, Microsoft and Anthropic said they will honour a single robots.txt signal for opting out of AI training. For anyone who publishes content, that is simpler than maintaining a list of crawler names. But rollout is uneven, the companies treat AI answers differently from training, and the signal is voluntary. Here is how to decide whether to use it, and how to check it works.

What was agreed

OpenAI, Google, Microsoft and Anthropic said on 30 October 2026 that they will honour one shared robots.txt token for opting out of AI training, so a single rule can cover all four companies' crawlers; the token is described in an IETF Internet-Draft14. It builds on the IETF's wider work on a shared vocabulary for AI usage preferences5.

The details matter more than the headline. Reporting says Anthropic has begun honouring the token, Microsoft is targeting early 2027 and separates training from grounding with a different tag, Google plans to treat the opt-out like its existing Google-Extended control, which also covers grounding for Gemini answers, and OpenAI has said it aims to implement it in the coming months13. Sources differ on some timings. The signal is voluntary and does not remove content already collected or models already trained2.

Training, grounding and search are three different decisions

The most important practical point is that "AI use" is not one thing. Training puts your content into a model. Grounding fetches your content at answer time and may cite and link you. Search indexing sends people to your site. Opting out of one may or may not affect the others, depending on the company.

For most businesses, the right answer differs by content type. Marketing pages and documentation usually benefit from being cited in AI answers. Paid research, proprietary datasets and member content usually should not train anyone's model for free. That is the same tiering logic as in what Reddit's logged-in move means for content.

A practical rollout

Keep the old rules for now. Until every company has implemented the shared token, keep existing company-specific crawler rules in place; reporting says those continue to work2.

Do not rely on signals for valuable content. Voluntary signals are honoured by companies that choose to honour them. Content that is your competitive asset needs technical controls at your edge, as described in the front-end controls and gateway patterns elsewhere on this site.

Verify, do not assume. Log requests by user agent and path at your edge or in your traces, and check that crawlers stop fetching opted-out sections after the relevant rollout dates. Enriching traces with request headers makes this a simple filter rather than a log-parsing project6.

A robots.txt line is a request, not a lock. Use it to state your preferences clearly, and use your own infrastructure for anything you cannot afford to lose.

Who should act

  • Publishers, marketplaces and data businesses: decide per content tier now, so the token goes in with a clear policy behind it.
  • B2B SaaS and documentation-heavy companies: think twice before opting out of grounding; being cited in AI answers is increasingly how buyers find you. See structured data for AI search.
  • Regulated organisations in the EU, UK and elsewhere: record your choices; they may matter for copyright and AI transparency discussions. See the Europe page.

CTO take

A single opt-out signal backed by four major AI companies is genuinely useful: it lowers the cost of saying no to training. But it arrives in stages, it does not cover the same things at each company, and it remains voluntary. Treat it as one part of a content access policy that separates training, grounding and search, enforces what matters at your edge, and verifies behaviour with your own data. Common questions

What did the four companies agree?

On 30 October 2026, OpenAI, Google, Microsoft and Anthropic said they will honour one shared robots.txt token for opting out of AI training, described in an IETF Internet-Draft. Rollout timing differs by company.

Does the token stop my content appearing in AI answers?

Not necessarily. Reporting says Microsoft separates training from grounding with a separate meta tag, while Google plans to treat the shared opt-out like its Google-Extended control, which also covers Gemini grounding. Check each company's documentation for your goals.

Does it remove content already used for training?

No. Coverage notes the signal is voluntary and applies going forward; it does not remove data already collected or change models already trained.
