From HTI-1 to HTI-5: how US health IT certification rules for AI are shifting, and what to build anyway

In 2023 US health IT regulation added detailed transparency duties for AI in certified EHRs. Two years later, a deregulatory proposal would remove them. For health tech teams, the lesson is not to build only what the rule of the moment requires.

US health IT certification rules shape what millions of clinicians see in their EHRs. In late 2023 the HTI-1 final rule made the most significant AI-related changes yet: transparency duties for predictive decision support. In late 2025 the HTI-5 proposed rule set out to remove many certification requirements, including those AI duties. Health tech leaders are understandably asking what they actually need to build.

This is an architect's reading. Confirm the current status of each rule and how it applies to you with regulatory counsel.

What HTI-1 did

  • USCDI v3 as the baseline. HTI-1 adopted the United States Core Data for Interoperability version 3 as the baseline standard from 1 January 2026, replacing version 11.
  • Decision support interventions. It established a decision support interventions (DSI) criterion and added a predictive DSI category covering AI and machine learning capabilities2.
  • Source attributes. Developers supplying predictive DSIs as part of certified health IT had to make detailed source attribute information available for review, often described as model card requirements, with an enforcement date of 1 January 2025 for the DSI provisions2.

What HTI-5 proposes

In December 2025 ASTP/ONC published HTI-5, a deregulatory proposed rule. Commentary describes it as proposing to remove 34 and revise seven of the 60 certification criteria56. ASTP/ONC's own materials describe the DSI change as reducing the criterion's scope to remove the AI model card requirements34. Some changes were proposed to take effect on publication of a final rule and others later; sources differ on the details5.

Holland & Knight characterised the year-end actions as a strategic pivot in federal health IT policy6. Check whether HTI-5 has been finalised, and in what form, before changing plans.

Regulation can remove a requirement to publish model information. It can't remove a hospital's need to understand the model it is relying on.

Why I'd keep building transparency anyway

Whatever happens to the certification criterion, the underlying questions don't go away:

  • Customers ask. Health systems' AI governance committees often ask vendors what data a model was trained on, how it performs in different populations, and how it is monitored. A clear model description shortens procurement.
  • Other regimes apply. Products sold internationally may face the EU AI Act, and regulated device software may fall under FDA pathways such as those in FDA PCCPs.
  • Safety and liability. When a model contributes to a harmful outcome, the organisation that can show what it knew about the model, and how it monitored it, is in a far stronger position. See AI incident postmortems.
  • Policy swings. Requirements removed in one cycle can return in another. Building transparency as a product capability is cheaper than switching it on and off.

What I'd build regardless of the rule

  1. A model description per predictive feature: intended use, training data sources, known limitations, performance overall and by relevant subgroups, update history. Keep it as structured data, not a PDF.
  2. Local validation before go-live at each customer, because performance varies between sites; federation helps, as in federated health analytics.
  3. Ongoing monitoring for drift and subgroup performance, with a defined path for clinicians to report problems.
  4. Change control: every model update versioned, evaluated against a fixed set, and recorded.
  5. Interoperable data foundations: USCDI-aligned FHIR data, which most predictive features depend on, built with the patterns in FHIR Bulk Data pipelines and TEFCA.

These map neatly to general frameworks too, such as those compared in ISO 42001 and the NIST AI RMF, so one body of evidence serves several audiences.

For health systems

If you buy rather than build, don't let a relaxed certification requirement relax your procurement. Ask vendors for the same information HTI-1 described, contractually, and require notification of model changes. Your governance committee's questions shouldn't depend on which rule is in force this year.

FAQ

Common questions

What did HTI-1 require for AI?

HTI-1 created a decision support interventions criterion, including a predictive DSI category covering AI and machine learning tools. Developers of certified health IT supplying predictive DSIs had to make detailed source attribute information available, often described as model card requirements.

What is the HTI-5 proposal?

A deregulatory proposed rule from ASTP/ONC published in late 2025 that would remove or revise a large share of health IT certification criteria. ASTP/ONC describes it as reducing the scope of the decision support interventions criterion to remove the AI model card requirements.

Is HTI-5 final?

It was published as a proposed rule. Check the Federal Register and HealthIT.gov for its current status before relying on any change, since proposals can be modified or withdrawn.
