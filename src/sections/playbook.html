Our AI pilot won't make it to production

Symptoms

Demos impress, but answers are unreliable, costs are unknown and nobody will sign off.

Diagnosis

Usually the model isn't the problem. Retrieval is ungrounded, there's no evaluation set, and no one owns quality.

My approach
  1. Define the decisions the AI supports and the cost of a wrong answer
  2. Ground retrieval in a domain ontology and knowledge graph (GraphRAG)
  3. Build an evaluation harness and tracing before adding features
  4. Orchestrate agents with LangGraph: explicit state, tools via MCP, human checkpoints
  5. Ship one workflow to production behind guardrails, then expand
What you get
Answers with citationsMeasurable quality gatesKnown cost per queryA path from pilot to platform

Our data is everywhere and means different things

Symptoms

Every team has its own definition of a customer, a patient or revenue. Reports disagree. AI makes it worse.

Diagnosis

It's a semantic problem, not a storage problem. Without a shared model, more pipelines just copy the confusion.

My approach
  1. Run domain workshops to capture core concepts and relationships
  2. Formalise an ontology and controlled vocabularies, versioned like code
  3. Map sources onto it: entity resolution and master data
  4. Expose a semantic layer to BI, APIs and AI agents
  5. Put data contracts and quality checks at the boundaries
What you get
One definition of truthReports that reconcileAI-ready knowledge graphFaster onboarding of new sources

Our platform buckles as we grow

Symptoms

Latency spikes at peak, batch jobs overrun, incidents are frequent and every release feels risky.

Diagnosis

Synchronous coupling and shared databases turn growth into contention. The architecture is fighting the load.

My approach
  1. Instrument first: find the real bottlenecks with tracing and load tests
  2. Set SLOs and latency budgets for the critical paths
  3. Move hot paths to an event-driven core with idempotent consumers
  4. Redesign data access: indexes, aggregations, read models, caching
  5. Automate deploys and rollbacks on Kubernetes with GitOps
What you get
1M+ tx/day headroomPredictable latencyFewer incidentsSafe, frequent releases

Our healthcare data won't interoperate

Symptoms

EHRs, labs and claims arrive in different formats and codes. Every integration is a bespoke project.

Diagnosis

Point-to-point integration doesn't scale. You need a standard exchange format and a standard analytical model.

My approach
  1. Inventory sources, code systems and PHI flows
  2. Standardise exchange on HL7 FHIR; normalise to SNOMED, LOINC and RxNorm
  3. Land analytics in OMOP CDM for reproducible research with OHDSI tools
  4. Build in de-identification, consent, encryption and audit trails
  5. Layer clinical AI only on governed, provenance-tracked data
What you get
Integrations in weeks, not monthsResearch-ready datasetsHIPAA-aligned by designTrusted clinical AI

Cloud costs and complexity are out of control

Symptoms

Environments were hand-built, nobody knows what's running, and the bill grows faster than revenue.

Diagnosis

Without infrastructure as code and cost visibility, every environment drifts and waste is invisible.

My approach
  1. Map every resource to an owner, a service and a cost
  2. Codify infrastructure in Terraform modules across AWS, Azure and GCP
  3. Consolidate workloads onto a shared Kubernetes platform
  4. Right-size, autoscale and route AI calls to the cheapest capable model
  5. Report unit economics per transaction, tenant and token
What you get
Reproducible environmentsLower, predictable spendAudit-ready infraPortable, no lock-in

We don't have senior technical leadership

Symptoms

The founders or executives are making technical bets without a trusted, experienced voice in the room.

Diagnosis

You don't need a full-time CTO yet. You need CTO-grade judgement on the decisions that are expensive to reverse.

My approach
  1. Assess the team, architecture, roadmap and risk in the first month
  2. Set a technical strategy tied to business milestones
  3. Establish architecture governance: ADRs, reviews, standards
  4. Hire and mentor senior engineers; design team topology
  5. Represent technology to the board, investors and partners
What you get
Confident technical decisionsA team that scalesInvestor-ready diligenceA clean handover to a full-time CTO
